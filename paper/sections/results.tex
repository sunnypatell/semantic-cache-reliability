\section{How often the proxy fails}
\label{sec:results}

We report separability first, because it does not depend on a chosen threshold, then the
false-hit/coverage trade-off (\cref{fig:frontier}), the score distributions that explain it,
and the operating points a deployment would actually run.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_frontier}
  \caption{The cost of safety. Each curve sweeps the similarity threshold for one encoder;
    the vertical axis is coverage (the fraction of queries served) and the horizontal axis

\subsection{Separability across models and domains}
\label{sec:results-auc}
\Cref{fig:heatmap} summarizes threshold-free separability as \prauc{} for every
encoder--domain pair. Two patterns hold across all six encoders. First, the domain matters
more than the model: \pending{the spread across domains for a fixed encoder is larger than
the spread across encoders for a fixed domain}. Second, the adversarial domain is close to
unsolved: \pending{the best encoder reaches only \prauc${}=X$ on \mbox{PAWS}, against a base
rate of $0.44$, and its \rocauc{} of $Y$ is barely above the $0.5$ of chance}. Larger
encoders help, but the gain is small next to the domain gap: \pending{moving from the
smallest to the largest model raises \mbox{PAWS} \prauc{} by only $Z$}. The lexical control
\pending{trails the neural encoders on questions and news but is competitive on
\mbox{PAWS}, where neither lexical overlap nor learned semantics separates the classes}.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\linewidth]{figures/fig_heatmap}
  \caption{Threshold-free separability (\prauc{}) for each encoder and domain. Higher is
    better; the base rate of equivalent pairs is $0.36$ (questions), $0.68$ (news), and
    $0.44$ (adversarial). The adversarial column stays near the floor for every encoder.}
  \label{fig:heatmap}
\end{figure}

\subsection{The false-hit/coverage frontier}
\label{sec:results-frontier}
\Cref{fig:frontier} reads the same data as an operating curve. To hold the false-hit rate
under $1\%$, a deployment must move far up the threshold, and coverage falls with it:
\pending{on questions the best encoder serves only X\% of traffic at a $1\%$ ceiling; on
news, Y\%; on the adversarial domain, essentially none}. The shape of the news frontier is
the most forgiving because its pairs are the least lexically confusable; the adversarial
frontier is the warning, because it is the case a real cache will meet whenever two prompts
differ in a small but decisive word.

\subsection{Why: the grey zone}
\label{sec:results-greyzone}
\Cref{fig:scores} shows where the failure comes from. For the strongest encoder, the
similarity scores of equivalent and non-equivalent pairs are well separated on questions
but pile into the same narrow, high band on the adversarial domain. \pending{On
\mbox{PAWS}, the median similarity of non-equivalent pairs is within $\delta$ of the median
of equivalent pairs, so no threshold can divide them.} A cache cannot act on a distinction
its encoder does not represent, and on adversarial near-duplicates the encoder does not
represent it.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_score_overlap}
  \caption{Similarity-score distributions for equivalent (blue) and non-equivalent (orange)
    pairs, for the strongest encoder, across the three domains. Separation on questions
    becomes near-total overlap on the adversarial domain, where both classes concentrate
    above $0.96$.}
  \label{fig:scores}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Operating points}
\label{sec:results-operating}
\Cref{fig:coverage} states the headline as a single number per encoder and domain: the
coverage a cache can deliver while keeping its false-hit rate at or below $1\%$.
\pending{Across encoders the achievable coverage at a $1\%$ ceiling is X--Y\% on questions,
A--B\% on news, and below C\% on the adversarial domain.} The practitioner's takeaway is
blunt: a single global threshold chosen for a clean domain will leak false hits in a harder
one, and there is no threshold that makes the adversarial domain both safe and useful.

    are $95\%$ bootstrap intervals. Even the strongest encoder serves only a small share of

\end{figure}
